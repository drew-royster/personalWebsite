# Personal site interview notes

Purpose: durable notes for shaping Drew Royster's personal website and the Ask Drew site agent. This site should feel like personal field notes / a dossier, not a sales page or lead-gen funnel.

## Positioning so far

- Drew does **not** want the site to be a marketing page.
- No aggressive calls to action.
- The site should be about Drew, what he is interested in, what he is building, and what he keeps thinking about.
- Desired reader reaction: **"This guy builds interesting practical stuff."**
- Tone to avoid:
  - AI hype guy
  - SaaS growth/marketing guy
  - enterprise consultant
  - crypto/web3 futurist
  - academic researcher cosplay
  - generic full-stack developer resume
- Better voice: curious builder, practical systems person, slightly skeptical, taste-driven, interested in real workflows and unfinished edges.

## Work / collaboration context

- Drew has done side work for cash in the past.
- Startups are fun.
- Drew would enjoy helping founders.
- Enterprise is probably not his jam.
- Drew is interested in SMBs/businesses that would pay for solid, useful agents, but the website should not be framed as a sales pitch.
- Earlier draft idea, not final copy: Drew is a generalist developer who can build effective and reliable agents for text and voice.
- Important refinement: don't over-market this; it should be available as context, not the homepage thesis.

## Common thread / interests

Drew likes trying to skate where the puck is going to be: building with technology that is **almost ready for prime time**.

What "almost ready" means:

- The model capability is newly possible.
- The hardware/interface is starting to make sense.
- The workflow pain is obvious and automatable.
- The tech is still janky enough that building useful systems requires taste.
- Not primarily because "the early weird version is more fun" — that framing is less true.

Candidate phrasing:

> Drew likes building with technology that is almost ready for prime time — close enough to be useful, unfinished enough that the interfaces, workflows, and failure modes still need to be invented.

## Personal texture

- Desired level of personal detail: mostly technical interests/projects, plus some origin story/personality and a little personal taste/hobbies.
- Drew is considering sharing books he has enjoyed as a safe way to reveal more personality without alienating or over-sharing.
- Possible presentation: a dedicated books page, a header/teaser, an About section, or a broader rearrangement around a "things I liked / things I am thinking about" narrative.
- Avoid making the books feel like a formal canon or generic recommendations list; they are personal texture / marginalia.
Books Drew is interested in / has been thinking about / may want represented as personal texture:

- That Hideous Strength — C. S. Lewis
  - Weird and good in the way only C. S. Lewis can be.
  - Drew is impressed by Lewis's power of discernment: his familiarity with the nuances of motivations and actions feels beyond most people today.
  - Valuable because Lewis simply sees more than we do.
- Watership Down — Richard Adams
  - Delightful and beautiful: a book of adventure and friendship.
  - Drew's aside: "We all live in Cowslip's Warren, by the way."
- Till We Have Faces — C. S. Lewis
  - How much of what we say is just jabbering noise? What questions will melt away when we meet the one with all the answers?
- Crime and Punishment — Fyodor Dostoevsky
  - Can someone go beyond good and evil?
  - Drew loves Dostoevsky's insights into the human heart.
- Beyond Good and Evil — Friedrich Nietzsche
  - What stuck with Drew was Nietzsche's brutal capacity to argue both sides of an argument forcefully.
  - One page can make an incredible case for evolution, totally convincing you, and the next page essentially says: "you're a moron if you believed me there."
  - Drew's read: Nietzsche never quite states his actual beliefs; he just likes fighting.
- There Is No Antimemetics Division — qntm
  - Most recent standout.
  - Drew finds it incredibly weird and dreadful in the true meaning of the word.
  - The idea that thoughts and things might not want to be discovered felt genuinely unimaginable/novel.
- Endurance — Alfred Lansing
  - Relentless optimism is what Drew took from this book.
  - Your mindset is something you decide; it is not really about the cards fate deals you.
  - The mission was an immediate disaster, yet Shackleton is known and respected for how he recovered from it without losing a man.
- Superluminary — John C. Wright
  - What if science was so integrated into our lives it seemed like magic?
  - That is what Superluminary feels like to Drew: incredible sci-fi that few have read.
  - The conclusion is an interesting answer to: "won't the cycle of war and death just repeat?"
